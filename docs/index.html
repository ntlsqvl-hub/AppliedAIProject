Policy Research Project

Disaster Recovery & Environmental Justice

How can federal disaster-recovery and restoration mechanisms address the disproportionate harm that extreme weather events impose on marginalized communities in the United States?

Prepared for state and local emergency management officials and legislators deciding how to allocate climate-resilience and disaster-recovery funding.

Data at a glance

This project combines federal disaster-funding records with independent measures of community vulnerability to see whether the two actually track each other. The table below summarizes every dataset currently in the project.

Primary data sources

The core quantitative evidence comes from OpenFEMA, the federal government's own disaster-funding records, compared against the Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool (CEJST) and EPA's EJSCREEN, which measure community vulnerability independently of any disaster program.

  • OpenFEMA Public Assistance and Hazard Mitigation funding, pulled directly from FEMA's open API.
  • CEJST Climate & Economic Justice Screening Tool, identifying disadvantaged census tracts.
  • EJSCREEN EPA's environmental justice indicators at the census block-group level.
  • Federal Register EPA rules and proposed rules referencing extreme weather and community impact.
